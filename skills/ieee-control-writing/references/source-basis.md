# Source Basis and Limits

## What this skill is based on

The initial corpus consists of two full-text papers from IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control consulted during a manuscript language-revision task. The selected sections were used to examine equation lead-ins, variable definitions, controller descriptions, and simulation-result reporting. This is a small representative corpus, not a frequency study of IEEE writing.

The skill combines three kinds of material:

1. Observed source wording: the two short excerpts below.
2. Editorial synthesis: original sentence frames and examples organized by control-writing function. These are not quotations or claims that every frame occurs verbatim in a source.
3. Author preferences from the originating task: common terminology, explicit objects, careful use of "hardware," content-preserving revision, and natural rather than mechanically varied sentence structure. These are editorial defaults, not IEEE requirements.

## R1. Reference-model and controller definitions

T. E. Gibson, Z. Qu, A. M. Annaswamy, and E. Lavretsky, "Adaptive Output Feedback Based on Closed-Loop Reference Models," IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control, vol. 60, no. 10, pp. 2728-2733, 2015.

[Author-hosted full text](https://stuff.mit.edu/people/tgibson/papers/teg_tac_2015.pdf) | [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2015.2405295)

Observed excerpt: "The reference system is defined as".

Sections II and V informed the model/control-definition and simulation-description patterns. The editorial lesson is to name the object before its defining equation and distinguish plant variables, reference-model variables, controller inputs, and adaptive updates.

## R2. Dynamics and estimation-error exposition

R. Kamalapurkar, B. Reish, G. Chowdhary, and W. E. Dixon, "Concurrent Learning for Parameter Estimation Using Dynamic State-Derivative Estimators," IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control, vol. 62, no. 7, pp. 3594-3601, 2017.

[Author-laboratory full text](https://ncr.mae.ufl.edu/papers/TAC17.pdf) | [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2017.2671343) | [Institutional publication record](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/concurrent-learning-for-parameter-estimation-using-dynamic-state-/)

Observed excerpt: "The dynamics of the model are described by".

Sections II, IV, and V informed the dynamics, error-definition, and simulation-result patterns. The editorial lesson is to identify the modeled object and its known or unknown quantities, then connect equations and reported errors to those named objects.

## What the quartile evidence does and does not establish

The [IEEE title list dated November 2023](https://open.ieee.org/wp-content/uploads/IEEE-Title-List-November-2023.pdf) lists IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control as Q1 in its JIF-quartile field. This is a dated journal-level classification. It is not a statement of the current Chinese Academy of Sciences division, the classification in either paper's publication year, or the quality of every article or sentence in the journal.

Use "IEEE control-paper style" or "TAC-informed writing patterns" as the skill's practical description. Do not promise that adopting these patterns makes a manuscript Q1-quality or improves its acceptance probability by a known amount. Novelty, technical soundness, evidence, and venue fit remain separate from sentence style.

## Extending the source corpus

Expand the corpus when the user requests additional style research or supplies relevant full texts. Do not browse for every routine sentence edit when the needed sources and facts are already available.

1. Select relevant primary sources, preferably publisher pages, institutional repositories, or author-hosted papers, and actually read the sections used as evidence.
2. Record bibliographic identity, a usable source link, and the sections examined. Separate an abstract-only observation from a full-text observation.
3. Extract the rhetorical function rather than copying paragraphs. Keep quotations brief and attributed; write new examples.
4. Mark new patterns as observed wording, editorial generalization, or user preference. Do not claim a phrase is common by frequency unless a reproducible frequency analysis was performed.
5. Check current journal classifications only when they are relevant to the user's request, using dated authoritative evidence and naming the classification system.

Do not add these style sources to a manuscript's scientific bibliography unless their scientific content is actually cited for a relevant reason. Do not distribute unpublished manuscript passages or experimental data as part of an expanded public skill without authorization.
